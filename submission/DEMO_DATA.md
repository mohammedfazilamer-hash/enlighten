## Demo Study Set Name

Photosynthesis in Two Minutes

## Screenshot or Paste Passage

Photosynthesis is the process plants use to store light energy as chemical energy. Inside chloroplasts, chlorophyll absorbs light. During the light-dependent reactions, water molecules are split, oxygen is released, and energy-carrying molecules are produced. The Calvin cycle then uses that stored energy to combine carbon dioxide into glucose. The plant can use glucose immediately during cellular respiration, convert it into building materials such as cellulose, or store it as starch. Light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration, temperature, water availability, and chlorophyll health can all affect the rate of photosynthesis. This process supports far more than the plant itself: it adds oxygen to the atmosphere and creates the energy-rich organic matter that begins most food chains.

## Tutor Question

Why would damaged chlorophyll reduce both plant growth and the oxygen released by the plant?

## Expected Explanation Themes

- The light-dependent reactions split water and release oxygen.
- The Calvin cycle uses carbon dioxide to build glucose.
- Glucose supports energy, growth, structure, and storage.
- Environmental conditions can limit the process.
- Photosynthesis supports atmospheric oxygen and food chains.
